# Module 0 — Orientation & AI Foundations for Finance
**Duration: 2.0 hours** · Part of *AI for Finance Professionals* (20-hr PBL course)

> Standalone facilitator brief. See `../course-design/delivery-methodology.md` for the session rhythm used in every module.

## Problem scenario
Two AI outputs are projected side by side. One is correct. The other contains a plausible but wrong total **and** an invented donor regulation. With no prior instruction, groups must decide which to trust — and justify why.

## Learning objectives
By the end, participants can explain what a generative model actually does, why it hallucinates, and why it is confidently wrong — and can state the course's governing rule.

## Concepts (introduced only as the problem demands)
- What a generative model is: pattern prediction, **not** calculation or database lookup
- Why it hallucinates and why confidence is not accuracy
- The difference between a chatbot and a spreadsheet
- Data privacy, first principles

## Hands-on
Each participant runs the *same* simple finance prompt and compares outputs across the room — surfacing that AI is non-deterministic and must be checked.

## Verification focus
Establish the course rule: **AI drafts, the professional signs.** Never the reverse.

## Deliverable
A personal **"trust ledger"** — a one-pager listing which tasks they'd let AI draft vs. never.

## Facilitator notes
Seed the two outputs yourself in advance. The whole point is that the room discovers the risk before anyone names it.
