# AI for Finance Professionals
### A 20-hour, problem-based learning course for the non-profit sector
*In-person or virtual delivery · Draft course design v1.0 · August 2026*

---

## 1. Why this course, designed this way

Across the sector, AI use is now near-universal but rarely transformative — a well-documented "efficiency plateau" where most teams run one-off prompts and never change how work actually gets done. In finance specifically, two problems dominate: **hallucinated numbers treated as fact**, and **"shadow AI"** — staff quietly pasting donor, salary, or beneficiary data into free tools with no controls.

So this course deliberately does *not* teach "AI in general." It teaches a small number of high-value finance workflows, each with a built-in **verify-before-you-trust** step and a **data-protection reflex**. That is the "narrow-and-governed" pattern the strongest nonprofit adopters use, and it is the difference between the 7% who see real impact and the rest.

Everything is built on **problem-based learning (PBL)**: every module opens with an authentic finance problem the participants recognise from their own desks. They diagnose it, solve it with AI, *check the AI's work*, and present. No lecture-first modules.

---

## 2. Who it is for

**Primary audience:** finance officers, accountants, grants-finance managers, and finance/operations directors in NGOs, INGOs, and community organisations.

**Prerequisites:**
- Comfortable with Excel/Sheets and email
- No prior AI experience assumed
- Own laptop; access to at least one general-purpose AI assistant (a free tier is fine)

**Group size:** 12–20 works best for PBL (small enough for facilitator to circulate, large enough for varied peer groups of 3–4).

---

## 3. What participants will be able to do (learning outcomes)

By the end, each participant can:

1. **Explain** what generative AI can and cannot reliably do in finance work, and name its two core risks (hallucination and data leakage).
2. **Accelerate** core finance tasks — budgets, donor reports, reconciliations, Excel work, policy questions — with a verification step applied every single time.
3. **Ground** AI in their own context (donor rules, local tax/VAT/TDS law, organisational policy) so outputs are actually usable, not generic.
4. **Apply** an organisational AI-use policy: what data may and may not be entered, who signs off, and how use is documented.
5. **Leave with** a personal, reusable "AI finance toolkit" — tested prompts and workflows for their real recurring tasks.

---

## 4. Delivery format and cadence

The course is **20 contact hours**. Three viable schedules — pick to fit the cohort:

| Format | Structure | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly (recommended)** | 10 sessions × 2 hrs over 10 weeks | PBL works best with spacing — learners apply to real work between sessions |
| **Intensive** | 5 days × 4 hrs | Travel-in cohorts, residential workshops |
| **Hybrid** | 3 in-person days + 2 virtual follow-ups | Geographically split teams |

**Recommendation:** the weekly/spaced version. The single biggest predictor of AI sticking is whether people use it on their *own* live tasks — spacing forces that.

**Virtual delivery notes:** use breakout rooms for the group-work block; a shared doc/whiteboard for the "diagnose" and "share" phases; screen-share for live prompting demos. Everything below works identically in-person and online.

### The PBL rhythm inside every 2-hour block

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 10 min | **Problem launch** | Facilitator presents the scenario. No solution hints. |
| 15 min | **Diagnose** | Groups define: what does a *good* answer need? What data? What could go wrong? |
| 55 min | **Work it** | Hands-on with AI, in pairs/small groups |
| 20 min | **Verify & critique** | Check the AI's numbers and claims against source. This is graded behaviour. |
| 20 min | **Share + reflect** | One group presents; debrief; each person logs one insight + one caution |

## 6. Module detail

### Module 0 — Orientation & AI foundations for finance *(1.5 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** Two AI outputs are projected — one is correct, one contains a plausible but wrong total and an invented donor regulation. Groups must decide which to trust and *why*, with no prior instruction.

**Concepts introduced (only as needed to solve the problem):** what a generative model is (pattern prediction, not calculation or lookup), why it hallucinates, why it is confidently wrong, the difference between a chatbot and a spreadsheet, data privacy first principles.

**Hands-on:** each person runs the same simple finance prompt in a general-purpose assistant and compares outputs across the room — surfacing that AI is non-deterministic and must be checked.

**Verification focus:** establishing the course rule — *AI drafts, the professional signs.* Never the reverse.

**Deliverable:** personal "trust ledger" — a one-pager listing which tasks they'd let AI draft vs. never.

---

### Module 1 — The trustworthy assistant: verification & data protection *(2 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** A finance officer pasted the full grant budget (with staff salaries and a beneficiary list) into a free consumer AI to "tidy the formatting." What are the exposures, and what should the organisation do — now and structurally?

**Concepts:** data classification (public / internal / confidential / personal), what "shadow AI" is and why it is the sector's top finance risk, human-in-the-loop review, prompt hygiene (redact, anonymise, use synthetic data), the difference between free consumer tools and enterprise/data-protected accounts.

**Hands-on:** take a real-looking (synthetic) donor budget and practise *de-identifying* it before use; build a personal red-line list of "never paste" data types.

**Verification focus:** the two-question gate before every prompt — (1) *Would I email this to a stranger?* (2) *Will I check the output against source?*

**Deliverable:** a one-page **Data Red-Line Card** for their desk.

---

### Module 2 — Building & stress-testing budgets *(2 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** A proposal budget is due in 48 hours. You have the donor template, a scope of work, and a staff grade scale — and a blank sheet.

**Concepts:** using AI to draft budget line structures, sense-check assumptions, generate justification narrative, and *pressure-test* (spot missing cost categories, unrealistic ratios, unallowable items).

**Tools:** general-purpose assistant for structure and narrative; spreadsheet-embedded AI (e.g., Copilot in Excel/Sheets AI) for formulas and totals — with the caution that **the AI must never be the source of the final arithmetic.**

**Hands-on:** groups build a modular grant budget from a scope of work, then ask AI to "act as a sceptical donor reviewer" and find weaknesses.

**Verification focus:** re-compute every total independently; confirm cost categories against the donor's actual budget notes, not the AI's assumptions.

**Deliverable:** a draft grant budget + AI-generated "reviewer critique" they addressed.

---

### Module 3 — Donor financial reports & narratives *(2 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** Quarterly financial report is due. The figures are final, but the variance narrative and the plain-language summary for a non-finance programme lead are not.

**Concepts:** turning numbers into narrative, explaining variances, tailoring the same financials to different audiences (donor / board / programme team), summarising long donor guidelines.

**Tools:** general-purpose assistants for drafting commentary and reformatting for different readers; note that these are widely regarded as the best all-round tools for drafting memos, reports, and summaries.

**Hands-on:** feed AI a (synthetic) budget-vs-actual table and produce three versions of the same variance explanation for three audiences.

**Verification focus:** every number in the narrative must trace back to the table; AI-written explanations of *why* a variance happened are hypotheses to confirm, never facts.

**Deliverable:** a three-audience variance narrative set.

---

### Module 4 — Excel & messy-data wrangling *(2.5 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** A bank/ledger export arrives with merged cells, inconsistent date formats, and duplicate rows. Reconciliation is tomorrow morning.

**Concepts:** using AI to write and *explain* formulas, clean and standardise data, build pivot summaries from plain-language instructions, and translate between "what I want" and the right function.

**Tools:** spreadsheet-embedded AI for in-context formula help; general-purpose assistants to explain or debug a formula and to generate cleaning steps.

**Hands-on:** each group receives the same deliberately messy dataset and races to a clean, reconciled summary — documenting every AI-assisted step so it's repeatable.

**Verification focus:** spot-check AI-written formulas on known values; never accept a formula you can't explain back.

**Deliverable:** a reusable "data-cleaning recipe" for their own recurring export.

---

### Module 5 — Compliance & policy interrogation *(2.5 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** A programme wants to charge a cost that may be unallowable under the donor's rules *and* may have a local tax/VAT/TDS implication. You need a defensible answer by end of day.

**Concepts:** the critical skill of **grounding** — AI does *not* reliably know your specific donor rules or your country's tax law, so you must *give it the source text* and ask it to reason within it. Retrieval over recall. This module is where participants learn to stop trusting the model's memory and start feeding it authoritative documents.

**Tools:** a citation-first research assistant (e.g., a tool that searches and cites live sources) for public regulatory questions; a document-grounded assistant where you paste/upload the actual donor guideline or tax provision and ask questions against *that text only*.

**Hands-on:** take a real (public) donor cost-principle document and a local tax provision; use AI to answer an eligibility question — then trace every claim back to a clause. Deliberately catch the AI inventing a rule.

**Verification focus:** no compliance answer leaves the room without a clause reference. If the AI can't cite it, treat it as unverified.

**Deliverable:** a worked eligibility determination with source clauses attached.

> **Note for the facilitator:** this is the highest-stakes module for finance credibility. Budget the full 2.5 hrs and expect strong engagement — this is where experienced staff realise AI is a *reasoning aid over their sources*, not an oracle.

---

### Module 6 — Bank reconciliation, partner transaction sampling & audit prep *(2.5 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** The quarter-end bank reconciliation has unexplained differences, three implementing partners have just submitted ledgers of several hundred transactions each, and the external audit is in three weeks. As grants accountant you cannot voucher every partner line, but you must sign off on partner spend and find your own problems before the auditor does.

**Part A — Bank reconciliation *(≈45 min)*:** using AI to match bank statement against cash book, list unmatched items on both sides, classify each reconciling item (outstanding payments, deposits in transit, unbooked charges, errors) and draft the adjusting entry — then **rebuilding the reconciliation statement independently** so adjusted bank balance equals adjusted book balance. Where ledger-embedded AI suggests matches, what it still gets wrong.

**Part B — Audit sampling of partner transactions *(≈60 min)*:** the core skill for grants accountants who review partner financial reports. Why 100% review is neither feasible nor expected; population, sampling unit and key-item threshold; the main approaches (risk-based/judgemental, random, stratified, monetary unit, systematic) and when each fits; sample size as a documented judgement driven by partner risk, prior findings and materiality; the per-item testing procedure (document exists, approved, arithmetically correct, in budget and period, donor-eligible, goods/services received, correctly recorded); and the documentation an auditor expects. AI drafts the sampling plan, stratifies the ledger, generates a **reproducible** selection (or the formula that does), produces the testing workpaper, and drafts the exceptions summary and partner follow-up letter. The accountant sets the risk rating and threshold, does the vouching, and reaches the conclusion.

**Part C — Anomaly spotting and audit readiness *(≈30 min)*:** drafting an audit-readiness checklist, describing ledger-wide patterns for AI to flag (duplicates, round-number cash payments, split payments under approval limits, post-period dates), and drafting management-response language. The anomaly sweep complements the sample; it does not replace it.

**Hands-on:** synthetic bank statement + cash book with seeded differences; a synthetic partner ledger (~600 lines) with seeded key items, a round-number cash cluster, post-period transactions and missing vouchers, plus a partner risk profile and a mini set of supporting documents to voucher against.

**Verification focus:** the rebuilt reconciliation must balance to zero or the AI's matching is wrong; the sample must capture the key items and the risky clusters, and must be reproducible on re-run; invented "standard" sample sizes or donor rules are challenged for a source; AI-flagged anomalies are leads, not conclusions. Vendor and staff names in partner ledgers are redacted before any real-world use.

**Deliverable:** a completed bank reconciliation with explained items; a **partner transaction sampling pack** (plan and rationale, reproducible selection, testing workpaper, exceptions summary, follow-up letter template); an audit-prep checklist + management-response paragraph.

---

### Module 7 — Forecasting, cash flow & scenarios *(2 hrs)*

**Problem scenario:** The board wants a six-month cash-flow projection with a base case and a "delayed disbursement" case, by next week.

**Concepts:** using AI to structure a cash-flow model, articulate assumptions, and generate scenario narratives; understanding that AI helps *frame and explain* forecasts but the drivers and figures are the professional's judgement.

**Hands-on:** groups build a simple two-scenario cash-flow projection and use AI to write the board-facing explanation of each scenario and its assumptions.

**Verification focus:** interrogate every assumption the AI proposes; label assumptions explicitly so the board sees judgement, not black-box output.

**Deliverable:** a two-scenario cash-flow projection + board narrative.

---

### Module 8 — Capstone + organisational AI-use playbook *(3 hrs)*

**Problem scenario (integrative):** Groups receive a realistic mixed brief — a grant closeout with a budget revision, a donor report, a compliance question, and a data-protection trap deliberately embedded. They must deliver a complete, verified package *and* explain how they kept it safe.

**Part A (2 hrs) — Capstone:** each group works the integrated problem end-to-end, applying every discipline from the course. Presentations + peer review against the rubric.

**Part B (1 hr) — The playbook:** the cohort co-drafts a lightweight **Organisational AI-Use Playbook** they can take back:
- 1–3 approved finance use cases to start with
- The data red-lines (what never goes in)
- The human sign-off step for each use case
- Who owns and monitors it (typically the finance/operations director)
- A simple log so use is visible, not shadow

This mirrors the "narrow-and-governed" model that distinguishes high-impact adopters.

**Deliverable:** completed capstone package + a first-draft AI-Use Playbook for their organisation.

## 7. Assessment (PBL-appropriate — no exam)

Finance professionals learn by doing, so assessment is **evidence of applied competence**, not recall:

1. **Portfolio (60%)** — the artifact produced in each module (red-line card, budget, variance narratives, cleaning recipe, eligibility determination, bank reconciliation and partner sampling pack, audit checklist, forecast). Assessed against a competency rubric.
2. **Capstone (30%)** — the integrated group deliverable + individual reflection.
3. **Verification discipline (10%)** — observed throughout: did they check the AI's work, protect data, and cite sources? This is graded *behaviour*, because it is the whole point.

**Competency rubric dimensions:** (a) task quality, (b) verification rigour, (c) data protection, (d) grounding in real sources/context, (e) appropriate judgement about when *not* to use AI.

## 8. Suggested tool stack (verify currency at delivery time)

The AI-tools landscape moves quarterly — treat any named product as illustrative and re-check before each cohort. As of August 2026, a sensible non-profit-budget stack:

- **General-purpose assistants** (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) — the all-round workhorses for drafting memos, reports, policies, quick calculations, and summarising documents. Free tiers are enough to learn on; a paid/enterprise tier is worth it where **data protection** matters.
- **Citation-first research** (e.g., Perplexity) — searches live sources and shows citations, useful for public regulatory/tax questions where traceability matters.
- **Spreadsheet-embedded AI** (e.g., Microsoft Copilot in Excel/Word/Outlook, or Sheets' AI) — best where the organisation already runs that ecosystem; strong for formulas, pivots, and first-draft reports without switching tools. Requires a licence.
- **Ledger-embedded AI** (e.g., QuickBooks Intuit Assist, Xero's assistant) — only relevant if the org already uses that accounting platform; handles reconciliation suggestions and anomaly flags inside the ledger.

**Deliberately out of scope for most small/medium nonprofits:** enterprise autonomous-finance platforms (order-to-cash, high-volume AP automation, SOX tooling). Name them so participants recognise them, but don't build the course on tools they won't have.

**The one rule that outlives every tool:** the professional verifies and signs; the AI drafts.

## 9. Facilitator preparation notes

- **Use synthetic data only in class.** Prepare realistic-but-fake datasets (budget, transaction export, bank statement + cash book, partner ledger with supporting vouchers, timesheet extract, donor guideline excerpt). Never bring real donor/beneficiary/salary data into the room — the course must model its own red-lines.
- **Pre-test every prompt** on the day's actual tools; outputs drift as models update.
- **Localise Module 5** to the cohort's real donors and tax jurisdiction (e.g., specific donor cost principles + local VAT/TDS/income-tax provisions). This is where the course earns credibility.
- **Seed the deliberate failures** — the invented regulation, the wrong total, the data-protection trap. Learning to *catch* AI is the highest-value skill here.
- **Keep groups mixed** by experience so senior finance staff and newer officers challenge each other.

## 10. What I'd confirm before finalising

A few choices I made as assumptions — worth your steer:
1. **Cadence** — I've recommended weekly over 10 weeks; confirm whether your cohort can do spaced delivery or needs an intensive.
2. **Jurisdiction & donors** — Module 5 should be localised to your specific tax regime and funders to be genuinely useful.
3. **Tool access** — whether participants will have licensed/data-protected accounts, or free tiers only (changes what you can safely demo).
4. **Certification** — whether this needs to carry a completion certificate or CPD hours, which would firm up the rubric.
